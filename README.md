# Let AI run the mess.

*Turning an existing bank or finance company AI-native*

https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/9f0d8595-99dc-4088-b696-6a53b428fdc4

## No big bang

A bank cannot close for a transformation. Loans are approved, payments collected and complaints answered every day while the change happens. So there is no big bang here, and no year of redesign before the first result. The same processes keep running while agents take them over, step by step. The old bank and the new one are the same bank, with no day in between.

This framework is for leaders of banks and finance companies, in Southeast Asia and Europe, who want to become AI-native without stopping the business. It names the decisions and gives my default for each. Thresholds, timing and budgets stay with each company.

> [!NOTE]
> **AI-native, as used here:** the default for any routine work is an agent. People decide, check, and deal with other people.
>
> Usually AI-native describes a company built that way from day one. Here it is the destination, not the birth.

## Four vectors

This framework moves four groups of people. Each row: what stops, and what starts.

| Vector | Old | New |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Leaders | Sponsor the programme | Go first |
| 2. Units | A central team builds for all | An ambassador in every unit |
| 3. Clients | Serve the client | Work inside the client's daily life |
| 4. Staff | Retrain or release | Staff become entrepreneurs |

The first two are known; most companies agree with them and few do them. The real change is in the last two, and in one idea inside vector 2: AI first, then clean up.

Staff is the last section in this text but the first step in time: the staff programme is announced before the first pilot.

## 1. Leaders

**Old: sponsor the programme. New: go first.**

### Go first

In most transformations the executive is a sponsor: signs the budget, opens the kick-off, chairs a steering committee once a quarter. But staff copy what leaders do, not what they fund. And an executive who has never had an agent draft a credit memo cannot tell a real proposal from a demo.

Now each executive uses AI in their own work every week, where others can see it: the board pack, the credit committee memo, the monthly results review, their own inbox.

One mechanism: at the weekly management meeting, two executives in turn each show one piece of their own work done with AI, two minutes each. What it saved, and what went wrong.

### One executive owns AI

One member of the executive team owns AI: a Chief AI Officer, or a deputy CEO with AI in the title. Not a project manager two levels down. The owner:

- sets the standards for data, security, models and cost;
- runs the central team and the shared platform;
- funds the chains (see vector 2); each chain owner answers for its P&L line;
- and has one job no one else has: getting the other executives to use AI in their own work. Sitting with each of them, finding the first task, fixing what breaks.

## 2. Units

**Old: a central team builds for all. New: an ambassador in every unit.**

### The ambassador

A central team builds for the units. Units queue for its time, and it knows the work less well than the people who do it.

Now every unit has one ambassador, from inside the unit:

- **Who:** the person colleagues already ask for help with a spreadsheet or a new system. Not necessarily the most senior.
- **Time:** up to 50% of working time, protected and written into their objectives.
- **Pay:** an allowance on top of salary, and a bonus tied to measured results in the unit.
- **Job:** turn the unit's needs into working AI: collect them, build the simple fixes, take the rest to the central team.

The central team stays small. Standards and platform come from the centre; the backlog comes from the units.

### Horizontal or vertical

There are two ways to roll AI out across units:

- **Horizontal:** the same tool for everyone. An assistant, document reading, call summaries.
- **Vertical:** one business chain rebuilt end to end. For example, a consumer loan from application to collection.

Horizontal is quick and builds habits. But the savings are thin and spread out: an hour saved per person per week rarely reaches the P&L. A 2025 report from MIT's Project NANDA found that tools such as ChatGPT and Copilot "primarily enhance individual productivity, not P&L performance".

My default: vertical leads, for three reasons.

1. **Waiting, not working.** At Kazkommertsbank (Qazkom), then Kazakhstan's largest bank, most of the 7 days a consumer-loan decision took was waiting between units, not work. A horizontal tool makes each unit faster, and the queues between them stay. A chain rebuilt end to end removes the queues.
2. **Money.** A chain moves one P&L line that can be named and measured: losses on bad loans, the cost to win a client, the cost to serve, income per client.
3. **Every unit, at small scale.** A consumer loan passes through sales, risk, operations, collections and finance. One chain pilot, on one product in one region, tests every unit at once, and each unit learns from its own part.

A vertical chain needs one owner with authority across units, named by the CEO. Without one, the chain stops at the first unit boundary. The ambassadors of each unit on the chain work with the owner.

Horizontal runs alongside, cheaply, for habits. The ambassadors run a pain mailbox. It collects the tasks people hate, not their jobs: "Send your most annoying routine task. Each month we pick the top 3, fix them within weeks, and show the results to everyone."

### Process or product

Horizontal or vertical decides the scope. Process or product decides what changes inside the chain, and a chain usually has both:

- **Process:** what staff do. Faster checks, fewer handoffs. The client sees only the result.
- **Product:** what the client gets. A new limit logic, a new channel, a new offer.

Process changes are cheaper to try and to undo. Product changes touch the client, so they carry consumer-protection review and reputation risk. My default: start each chain with process, and keep one visible product change alongside, so the board and the clients see progress. For the product, take a task the client already does and make it better; don't reinvent the product.

### AI first, then clean up

For more than thirty years the rule was: map the process, redesign it, standardise it, then automate it. With rigid software, that was the only order that worked. I worked this way myself: at Qazkom we cut the consumer-loan decision from 7 days to 20 minutes by streamlining the process first and automating it afterwards.

The rule has two costs. The redesign takes months before the first result. And the map is wrong: it shows the formal process and misses the informal one. The call to a colleague, the spreadsheet on someone's desktop, the exception everyone knows how to handle.

AI agents open another option. An agent reads the same emails, scans, spreadsheets and screens that people read, so it can run the process as it is today, informal parts included, with a person still pressing the final button in the core system until the agent has a proper interface to it. In 1990 Michael Hammer titled his Harvard Business Review article ["Reengineering Work: Don't Automate, Obliterate"](https://hbr.org/1990/07/reengineering-work-dont-automate-obliterate). With agents, automating can be how you obliterate.

Three steps:

1. **Absorb.** The agent runs the process as it is, at small scale. A person checks its output. The old way runs alongside until the numbers match.
2. **Log.** The agent records every step it takes. Its log becomes the real process map, informal steps included, with no mapping project.
3. **Clean.** Every quarter, delete the steps that added nothing: handoffs, re-typing, approvals that only checked someone else's typing. Turn the stable parts into plain rules or scripts, which are cheaper and give the same answer every time. Not everything needs AI, and the agent shows which parts don't.

Cleaning goes on the calendar, not on "later". An agent can make a wasteful process cheap enough that nobody ever cleans it.

**When the old order still wins:**

- **The data is wrong at the source.** An agent working on wrong data makes wrong decisions faster. Fix the source first.
- **A control exists on purpose.** A second signature on credit, a regulatory check. It is redesigned deliberately, never deleted in a log review.
- **The process should not exist at all.** If a step only feeds a report nobody reads, delete it before any agent learns it.

## 3. Clients

**Old: serve the client. New: work inside the client's daily life.**

A lender usually sees its clients a few times a year, through what they choose to report. Now it works inside their daily life, as deep as they allow. This applies to business clients, such as a small shop, a supplier or a retail partner that sells the lender's loans, and to individual borrowers.

### AI employees, hired by business clients

The lender offers its business clients AI employees, each with a plain job title:

- **Invoice clerk:** issues invoices, reminds customers to pay, matches payments.
- **Bookkeeper:** keeps the books, prepares the figures for tax filings.
- **Stock keeper:** tracks stock and reorders.

The client hires them the way it would hire staff: chooses the role, sees the work, can let them go.

Where the banking licence does not allow this, the lender offers it through a subsidiary or a partner, and says so to the client. Finance companies usually have more room than banks.

The AI employee comes first, before any loan, doing the routine the owner most wants off their desk: payments, invoices, bookkeeping. Financing follows when the owner needs it.

For the same idea applied to invoice financing, see the AI employee on both sides of an invoice in [Fund the cycle, not the relationship](https://github.com/ernar77id/invoice-financing-framework).

### An AI assistant for individuals

For an individual borrower, the same idea is a personal AI assistant in the lender's app:

- **Bills:** reminds of due dates and lines them up with payday.
- **Budget:** shows where the money went, and warns before a payment would bounce.
- **Paperwork:** fills in forms, collects documents, compares offers such as insurance or phone plans.

A borrower who sees the instalment coming, with the money planned for it, misses fewer payments. In a [trial in Uganda](https://www.nber.org/papers/w17020), simple text reminders improved on-time payment about as much as a 25 percent rate cut. And when the signs of money stress appear, the lender can help before the first missed payment, not after.

### What the lender gains

- **Clients met earlier.** The AI arrives before the loan, so the lender knows the client before the first credit decision, and before its competitors do.
- **Risk seen early.** With the client's consent, the lender sees how the money actually moves, every day: a shop's sales and stock, a household's income and bills. A problem shows up in the month it starts, not at the first missed payment.
- **A client that manages its money better repays better.** The AI does work the client could not afford a person for.
- **A moat.** A competitor can match a rate in a day. It cannot easily replace a bookkeeper that has kept a shop's books for two years, or the assistant that has planned a family's bills.

The moat must come from usefulness, not from lock-in. A client that cannot leave will resent the lender, and regulators will notice.

| Risk | Guardrail | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| The AI serves the lender, not the client | The client owns its data and can switch the AI off at any time | It does real work for the client first; the lender sees only what the client agrees to share |
| The client feels pushed into it | Never a condition of a loan or a better price | Offered before any financing, for daily work |
| Lock-in instead of loyalty | The client leaves with all its data, in a standard format | The client stays because leaving costs it help it relies on, not because it is trapped |
| The lender fixes everything | A fixed list of roles | Runs inside software the client already uses, where possible |
| The AI makes a costly mistake | A liability limit, agreed before the client hires it | Large filings and payments confirmed by the client first |
| Client data drifts into credit decisions | Credit use needs a separate agreement; the client can refuse it and keep the AI | The client sees the same data the lender sees |

Most clients will decline unless the AI saves them real work or worry. Plan for that.

## 4. Staff

**Old: retrain or release. New: staff become entrepreneurs.**

### Why it comes first, not last

When work is automated, the usual options are retraining for another internal job, of which there are few, or a severance package.

Agents will absorb a large part of the routine work in a bank, and some roles will end. Saying otherwise loses trust. And "AI first, then clean up" works only if staff show the agent how the work is really done: the informal steps, the exceptions, the workarounds. Staff who fear for their jobs hide exactly that.

So the people programme is a condition of the transformation, not a cost after it. Announce it before the first pilot, not when the first role ends. The message to staff is simple: the programme is open to everyone, and nobody loses a role to AI without first being offered a new role or the salary bridge below.

The logs record the process, not the person: they are never used to rate an individual. Where there is a works council or a union, this is agreed in writing before the first agent reads a desktop.

### Learn, build, leave gladly

The company lets its people learn new things, build a business or a side project while employed, and leave on good terms.

1. **Learn.** Time to study, on company time: running a small business, selling, pricing, cash, and working with AI from day one. Taught by people who have run small businesses, not only by trainers.
2. **Build.** Permission to build a business or a project while still employed, declared to the company, with the guardrails below.
3. **A salary bridge.** For up to 6 months, the person works part time on full salary: half the day handing their work over, to the agents or to a colleague, the other half on their own business, in the company's space, with help from its AI team.
4. **A first loan.** The company knows the person's work record and can lend to the new business, starting small, under its staff-lending rules.
5. **First clients.** The company can buy from the new business on normal terms, and introduce it to its own clients who need what it sells.
6. **Leave gladly.** When the bridge ends, the person leaves with their own business, not a severance letter. Some stay close as partners: sales agents, retail partners, service providers. Some become clients, with AI employees of their own (vector 3).

Priority goes to people in roles the agents will absorb, and the first group is volunteers. Internal roles come first for those who want to stay: agent supervisor, exception handler, ambassador, a place on a chain team.

### How roles change

Most of the change is absorbed by the first two steps; the bridge is for those who want to go.

1. **Don't replace leavers** in roles the agents can do. In high-turnover units, such as call centres and collections, this alone absorbs much of the change.
2. **Move people to units that grow.** With AI each person does more, so the units that sell and serve clients can grow with the people the bank already has: sales, client service, helping clients start with their AI employees (vector 3).
3. **Open the bridge to anyone.** A volunteer from a unit the agents have not reached frees a seat for someone whose work was absorbed.
4. **Only then, the normal process,** with the bridge offered first. Whoever takes the bridge leaves on their own terms; whoever does not receives what the law requires.

### What it costs

The bridge pays six months' full salary, and half of those hours still go to the company's work. The real cost is about three months' salary per person. It buys the cooperation the transformation needs.

| Risk | Guardrail | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Conflict of interest | No business that competes with the company or serves its clients without approval | Every side business is declared to compliance; purchases from it go through normal procurement |
| Client data leaves with the person | No client data, no company tools in the side business | The same access controls as for any leaver; the side business gets its own AI employees |
| Loans to staff businesses go bad | Normal credit rules; a loan is never a condition of the bridge | A small first limit that grows with the repayment record |
| The best people leave | Internal roles offered first | Ambassadors and chain teams drawn from the people who know the work |
| The new business fails | The bridge is chosen, never assigned | For 12 months after leaving, the person has priority for open roles |

## Governance and security

"AI first, then clean up" gives agents wider access than any system before them. They read emails, scans and the spreadsheets on people's desktops, and much of that text was written by someone outside the bank. Governance decides what agents may do; security makes sure nobody else can make them do more.

These rules match where regulators are going. The EU AI Act treats credit scoring of individuals as high-risk, GDPR gives borrowers the right to ask for a human review, Singapore's MAS expects an inventory of AI systems and board oversight, and DORA sets what a vendor contract must contain.

### Governance

- **One owner.** The executive who owns AI (vector 1) answers to the board for it. The board reviews AI risk every quarter, alongside credit and liquidity.
- **An agent register.** Every agent in production is listed: what it does, what data it reads, what it may decide, who owns it, when it was last reviewed. An agent that is not in the register does not run.
- **Three levels of decision.** The agent acts alone; the agent proposes and a person approves; only a person decides. Small, standard credit decisions may be made by an approved model alone, inside limits set by the credit committee, and the client can always ask for a person. Larger or unusual ones need a person, to approve or to decide.
- **Checks in proportion to impact.** Each agent is tested before it goes live and monitored after. Agents that touch credit, pricing or collections also go through the bank's model validation function before launch and internal audit afterwards, and their outcomes are tested for fairness across customer groups.
- **A log for every decision.** What went in, what came out, who approved it. Kept as long as the credit file it belongs to.
- **Customer data.** Client data stays where the law requires. Data from clients' AI employees stays separate from credit data unless the client agrees (vector 3).
- **Vendors.** The contract says how the vendor may use the bank's data (not to train its models on client data), where the data is stored, who may subcontract, how incidents are reported, how the bank audits the vendor, and how the bank leaves with its data and its prompts.
- **The supervisor hears it first.** Material AI uses are explained to the regulator before they go live, not after a complaint.

### Security

| Risk | Guardrail | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| An agent does more than its job | Each agent has its own identity and only the access its job needs; no shared logins | Access reviewed with the agent register, every quarter |
| Hidden instructions in what the agent reads | Text inside an email, scan or file is data, never a command | The agent can act only through approved actions; anything outside them goes to a person |
| An agent moves money by mistake or under attack | Hard limits: without a person, no payout above a set amount and no change to a client's bank account | Limits enforced in the payment system, not only in the agent |
| An agent fails or is compromised | Every agent can be stopped within minutes | The work falls back to people; during "absorb" the old way is still running, so the fallback is real |
| Fraud made with AI: fake voices, fake IDs, fake "our bank account has changed" emails | No change of payment details or identity on one channel alone | Confirmation through a second channel the bank already knows |
| Weak spots nobody tested | A red team attacks each agent before launch | And again after every major change, and at least once a year |

## Measurement

- **A baseline before every pilot:** time, cost, errors, losses. No numbers, no scaling.
- **Money, not hours.** Freed hours count only when they change hiring, cost or income.
- **One page for the board each month:** each chain and its P&L line against the baseline, each unit's backlog, the leaders' own use, the clients using AI employees and assistants, the staff on the bridge, and any AI incident.
- **Staff:** businesses started and still trading after 12 and 24 months, their loans and repayment, and whether staff keep using the pain mailbox. If they stop, trust has gone.

### Four checks: is it AI-native yet?

1. **Leaders.** Every executive can show their own AI work from last month.
2. **Units.** Every unit has an ambassador with protected time, and at least one chain runs end to end with agents and beats its baseline.
3. **Clients.** Clients use the lender's AI employees and assistants daily, including some that have never borrowed.
4. **Staff.** Everyone whose work was absorbed has been offered a path, and the company knows where they went.

## The first 90 days

**Days 1 to 30: start.**

- Name the executive who owns AI. Leaders start using AI in their own work.
- Announce the staff programme, before any role changes.
- Name the ambassadors and protect their time. They open the pain mailbox in their units.
- Choose the first chain by the P&L line it moves, and name its owner.
- Start the agent register and set the three levels of decision.
- Take the baselines.
- Brief the supervisor; start the vendor contract, the impact assessments, and talks with the works council or union where there is one.

**Days 31 to 60: absorb.**

- A red team attacks the chain's agents before they touch live work.
- Agents run the chain at small scale in shadow mode: a person checks every output, and the old way still decides.
- The first three mailbox fixes ship.
- The first staff group starts learning.

**Days 61 to 90: measure and decide.**

- Compare against the baseline, and run the first clean-up round on the logs.
- Decide: scale the chain, fix it, or stop it.
- Show the numbers to everyone, including what failed.

**After 90 days:** a new chain each quarter, a clean-up round each quarter, and AI employees and assistants offered to the first clients.

## The day nobody noticed

Customers should never notice the day the old bank died. They notice that the loan came faster and the questions were fewer. Staff notice that the work they disliked is gone, and that the company told them early what comes next.

Let AI run the mess. Then clean it up.
